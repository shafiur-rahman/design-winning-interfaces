# Experience architecture

Use this to turn the product goal into a page narrative or task flow before styling.

## Define the job

Write:

- Job: When [situation], the user wants to [motivation], so they can [outcome].
- Success signal: the observable user or business outcome.
- Main risk: what could prevent trust, comprehension, or completion.
- Primary action: the single next step that advances the job.

## Build the content hierarchy

Rank content in four levels:

1. Must understand immediately.
2. Must understand before acting.
3. Useful for comparison or reassurance.
4. Optional detail available on demand.

Give each viewport one dominant idea. Use progressive disclosure for expert detail, not hidden essential information.

## Map the journey

For a task flow, use:

| Stage | User intent | Needed information | Primary action | System feedback | Risk and recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

For a marketing page, use this narrative:

1. Orient: identify the offer and relevant audience.
2. Motivate: make the outcome concrete.
3. Explain: show how the product works.
4. Prove: reduce risk with credible evidence.
5. Differentiate: clarify why this choice is meaningfully better.
6. Resolve: answer objections and constraints.
7. Act: present the right commitment at the right moment.

Reorder or remove stages when the user already has high intent.

## Define the interface grammar

Specify:

- global navigation and location cues;
- page and section hierarchy;
- primary and secondary actions;
- repeated components and their rules;
- dense versus spacious regions;
- what becomes fixed, collapses, reorders, or disappears at each breakpoint.

Use cards only when items are independently actionable, comparable, selectable, or movable. Do not use cards as the default container for ordinary prose.

## Plan responsive priority

At each meaningful breakpoint, decide:

- what remains above the fold;
- what reorders;
- what converts from parallel to sequential;
- what becomes a menu, disclosure, drawer, table, or horizontal scroller;
- which media crops or changes;
- how touch targets and reading measure remain comfortable.

Do not solve mobile by scaling the desktop composition down.
