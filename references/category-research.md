# Category research

Use this before substantial greenfield design, redesign, or category-sensitive critique.

## Research set

Collect only enough evidence to make decisions:

1. Inspect the product’s own site, application, brand assets, analytics or research supplied by the user.
2. Review three to five current direct competitors.
3. Review two or three adjacent products that solve a similar trust, complexity, or conversion problem.
4. Review one or two editorial or award examples for craft, not category convention.
5. Check relevant platform conventions and accessibility requirements.

Prefer first-party product pages, live interfaces, official documentation, and dated case studies. Use galleries and social posts as discovery aids, not sole evidence.

## Evidence board

Record compact observations in this form:

| Source and date | Observed pattern | Convention, trend, or outlier | User expectation served | Opportunity for this product |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Capture facts, not adjectives. “Pricing comparison appears before FAQ on four of five sites” is useful. “Clean and premium” is not.

## Extract five layers

### Category grammar

Identify familiar navigation, content order, terminology, trust signals, interaction models, and information density. Preserve conventions that reduce learning cost.

### Visual saturation

Identify overused palettes, typography, illustration styles, layouts, and motion tropes. Treat high prevalence as a warning against imitation, not automatic validation.

### Trust mechanics

Identify what reduces perceived risk: demonstrations, transparent pricing, security evidence, social proof, methodology, comparison, guarantees, or human support.

### Audience reality

Identify device context, accessibility needs, time pressure, expertise, cultural expectations, and the consequences of mistakes.

### White space in the market

Find an underused but credible combination of voice, composition, interaction, or visual metaphor. Differentiate through a product truth, not novelty alone.

## Synthesis

Produce a short research snapshot:

- three conventions to honor;
- three saturated patterns to avoid;
- two trust mechanisms to strengthen;
- one differentiation opportunity;
- one open risk or assumption.

Translate the evidence into principles. Never reproduce another product’s layout, wording, illustration, iconography, animation, or brand identifiers.
